## Three findings

- The Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist covers Sep 2016 to Oct 2018, with partial months in Sep 2016, Oct 2016, Dec 2016, Sep 2018 and Oct 2018. It contains 96,478 delivered orders and 13,221,498 revenue.
- Lateness is associated with lower reviews: late orders averaged 2.27 versus 4.29 for on-time orders; low reviews were 62.4% versus 9.3%. Lateness and reviews are correlated, not proven cause and effect. The late rate is 6.8% by one measure and 8.1% by timestamp.
- Repeat rate was 3.0%. The top3 categories contributed 25.9% of revenue, while freight was 16.6% of revenue.

## Three recommendations

- Prioritize delivery improvements in AL, MA, SE, PI, CE. **Expected impact:** the what-if estimates 286 late orders and 152 low reviews avoided. **Assumption:** targeting these 5 states can reproduce the what-if results.
- Track delivery delays alongside reviews and investigate orders as delays grow. **Expected impact:** focus on the delay buckets associated with lower reviews, including 8-14 days late (1.67). **Assumption:** reducing delays may improve reviews, though the observed relationship does not establish causality.
- Test initiatives to encourage later repeat purchases. **Expected impact:** each repeat-rate point is associated with 934 extra repeat customers and 127,939 revenue. **Assumption:** the estimate applies to future customer behavior.

## Risks

Partial months may affect comparisons. Delivered orders without a delivery date: 8; with a delivery date before the purchase date: 0 (both left out of lateness metrics). Products without a category: 610. Late-rate measures differ, so clarify the definition before using them for performance targets.

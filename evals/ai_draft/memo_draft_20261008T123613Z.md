## Three findings

- Across Sep 2016 to Oct 2018, the dataset records 96,478 delivered orders and revenue of 13,221,498. The period includes partial months.
- Lateness and reviews are correlated, not proven cause and effect. Late orders have an average review of 2.27 versus 4.29 for on-time orders; the review gap is 2.02 (CI: 1.98–2.06). The reported late rate is 6.8%, while the timestamp-based rate is 8.1%.
- Repeat purchasing is limited: the repeat rate is 3.0%. Freight accounts for 16.6% of revenue, and the top 3 categories account for 25.9% of revenue.

## Three recommendations

- **Prioritize delivery reliability in the worst states.** Expected impact: the scenario for 5 states shows 286 late orders avoided and 152 low reviews avoided. **Assumption:** the modeled state-level intervention achieves its projected effect.
- **Test a repeat-purchase follow-up.** Expected impact: 934 extra repeat customers and revenue of 127,939 per repeat-rate point. **Assumption:** the stated per-point estimates apply to the follow-up and customer behavior responds as expected.
- **Review freight costs in high-freight categories.** Expected impact: freight reduction of 32,979. **Assumption:** the proposed changes can achieve the provided reduction without materially affecting sales or service.

## Risks

Partial months may affect comparisons. Delivered orders without a delivery date (8) and orders with a delivery date before purchase (0) are left out of lateness metrics; products without a category (610) may affect category analysis. Lateness and reviews are observationally associated, so operational changes should be evaluated before attributing review improvements to delivery performance.

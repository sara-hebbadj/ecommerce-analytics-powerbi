## Three findings

- The dataset covers Sep 2016 to Oct 2018, including partial months: Sep 2016, Oct 2016, Dec 2016, Sep 2018, and Oct 2018. It records 96,478 delivered orders and 13,221,498 in revenue.
- The late rate is 6.8%; a timestamp-based measure reports 8.1%. Late orders averaged 2.27 in reviews versus 4.29 for on-time orders, a review gap of 2.02 (CI: 1.98–2.06). **Lateness and reviews are correlated, not proven cause and effect.**
- Repeat rate is 3.0%, while repeat rate by customer ID is 0.0%. Freight is 16.6% of revenue; the top three categories account for 25.9%.

## Three recommendations

- Pilot delivery interventions in the five worst-performing states. **Expected impact:** the what-if estimates 286 late orders and 152 low reviews avoided. **Assumption:** targeted actions achieve the modeled improvement; this is not evidence that lateness causes low reviews.
- Improve repeat purchasing measurement and test retention initiatives. **Expected impact:** each repeat-rate point is associated with 934 extra repeat customers and 127,939 in revenue. **Assumption:** the rate can be lifted and the associated customers and revenue materialize.
- Review freight pricing and operations in high-freight categories. **Expected impact:** the modeled freight reduction is 32,979. **Assumption:** category-level changes can reduce freight without harming conversion or delivery performance.

## Risks

- Eight delivered orders lack a delivery date and are excluded from lateness metrics; products without a category: 610.
- Partial months may distort comparisons. The difference between late-rate measures and the repeat-rate definitions warrants validation before setting targets.
- The state and freight impacts are modeled, not guaranteed; test interventions before scaling.

## Three findings

- The dataset covers Sep 2016 to Oct 2018, with partial months in Sep 2016, Oct 2016, Dec 2016, Sep 2018, and Oct 2018. It includes 96,478 delivered orders and 13,221,498 in revenue.
- Late orders had an average review of 2.27, compared with 4.29 for on-time orders; low reviews were 62.4% for late orders and 9.3% for on-time orders. **Lateness and reviews are correlated, not proven cause and effect.**
- Repeat customers were 2,801, with a repeat rate of 3.0%. Freight accounted for 16.6% of revenue; the top three categories contributed 25.9% of revenue.

## Three recommendations

- **Prioritize delivery interventions in AL, MA, SE, PI, and CE.** Expected impact: the what-if analysis estimates 286 late orders and 152 low reviews avoided. **Assumption:** interventions in these states can achieve the what-if scenario’s effects.
- **Test retention efforts that encourage later repeat purchases.** Expected impact: per point of repeat-rate improvement, 934 extra repeat customers and 127,939 in revenue. **Assumption:** the reported per-point relationship holds as retention improves.
- **Review freight costs in high-freight categories.** Expected impact: freight reduction of 32,979. **Assumption:** category-level changes can realize the reported reduction without unacceptable effects on sales or delivery.

## Risks

- The period includes partial months, which may affect comparisons across time.
- Delivered orders without a delivery date: 8; with a delivery date before the purchase date: 0. Both were left out of lateness metrics. Products without a category: 610.
- The what-if impacts are estimates, and the review relationship does not establish that lateness causes lower ratings.
